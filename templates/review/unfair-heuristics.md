> Always loaded by `/research.review`, whatever the venue. Distilled from the reviewer
> guidelines major venues publish; these are the reflexes those guidelines tell reviewers to
> stop using, because they fire on almost any paper and so separate nothing.

# Criticisms that are not findings

A weakness that could be written about most submissions is not a weakness, it is a reflex. Each
one below is either **drop it**, or **convert it** into the specific version that would survive
an author's rebuttal. If you cannot convert it, it does not go in the review.

## Novelty reflexes

| Reflex | Why it fails | Convert to |
|---|---|---|
| "Obvious in hindsight" | Everything is, once shown. Hindsight bias. | What in the paper's own framing was already known? Name the prior work. |
| "Not novel" with no citation | An assertion, not evidence. | Name the paper, its venue, and its date, and say which specific claim it pre-empts. |
| "Too novel" / unfamiliar framing | Penalises the work that is hardest to publish. | Nothing. Drop it. |
| "Too simple" | A simpler mechanism that works is better, not worse. | Does the simplicity hide an untested assumption? Name it. |
| "Too narrow" | Narrow scope done properly beats broad scope done loosely. | Does the paper claim more breadth than it evaluated? That is overclaim, a different finding. |
| "Few citations in this area" | Citation count is not validity. | Nothing. Drop it. |

## Evidence reflexes

| Reflex | Why it fails | Convert to |
|---|---|---|
| "Does not beat the state of the art" | Not necessary and not sufficient for a contribution. | Does the paper *claim* to beat it and not show it? Then it is an overclaim. |
| "I expected the opposite result" | Confirmation bias. Evidence drives the conclusion. | Is the result under-evidenced *as presented*? Say how. |
| "Negative result, so not interesting" | Negative results are how publication bias gets corrected. | Nothing. Drop it. |
| "Needs experiment X to be accepted" | Extra experiments are a suggestion, not a bar. | Only a finding if a **stated claim** cannot stand without X. Say which claim. |
| "Did not compare against [newest closed model]" | Only relevant if a claim rests on that comparison. | Name the claim that requires it. |
| "Missing reference" that is a recent preprint | Unrefereed and recent work is a suggestion, not a requirement. | Offer it as a pointer, explicitly not as a weakness. |

## Framing reflexes

| Reflex | Why it fails | Convert to |
|---|---|---|
| "I would have done it differently" | Review the paper written, not the one you would write. | Only a finding if their choice **prevents them answering their own question**. |
| "Writing is not polished" | Clarity is the bar, not style. | Point at a specific passage you could not resolve on one pass. |
| "They admit limitation X" | An acknowledged limitation is honesty, not a weakness. | Only a finding if the limitation invalidates a core claim. |
| "Not a systems/model paper" | The field takes resources, measurements, analyses, surveys, position papers. | Nothing. Drop it. |
| "Motivation is weak" | The author cannot tell whether you mean the problem or the presentation. | Which one, and what specifically would fix it. |

## The test

Before a weakness goes in the review, it must read:

```
[what] is [weak / unsupported / unclear] because [concrete cause] ([where in the paper]);
fix: [concrete change].
```

If the bracket for *where in the paper* cannot be filled, you are reviewing an impression.
